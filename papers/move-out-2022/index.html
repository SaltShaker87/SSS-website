MOVe-OUT: Molnupiravir for Oral Treatment of Covid-19 in Nonhospitalized Patients

background.txt

Background

New treatments were needed to stop mild Covid-19 from progressing, ideally pills people could take at home. Molnupiravir is an oral, small-molecule antiviral prodrug active against SARS-CoV-2. MOVe-OUT was the phase 3 trial that tested whether 5 days of molnupiravir, started early, could keep at-risk, unvaccinated outpatients out of hospital.

methods.txt

Methods

DesignPhase 3, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial, with a planned interim analysis once 50% of the 1550-participant target had been followed through day 29.
PopulationNonhospitalized, unvaccinated adults with mild-to-moderate, laboratory-confirmed Covid-19, signs or symptoms for no more than 5 days, and at least one risk factor for severe illness. 1433 participants underwent randomization (716 participants molnupiravir, 717 participants placebo).
InterventionMolnupiravir 800 mg by mouth twice daily for 5 days, started within 5 days of symptom onset.
ComparatorMatching placebo twice daily for 5 days.
Primary endpointHospitalization for any cause or death through day 29 (efficacy); incidence of adverse events (safety).
Follow-upThrough day 29.

The interim analysis showed superiority, and the trial then reported the analysis of all randomized participants. The effect shrank between the two looks, which is the main thing to understand about this trial.

results.xls

Results

At the interim analysis, hospitalization or death through day 29 occurred in 7.3% with molnupiravir versus 14.1% with placebo (difference −6.8 percentage points). In the analysis of all randomized participants, the gap was smaller: 6.8% versus 9.7% (difference −3.0 percentage points; 95% CI −5.9 to −0.1).

“the percentage of participants who were hospitalized or died through day 29 was lower in the molnupiravir group than in the placebo group (6.8% [48 of 709] vs. 9.7% [68 of 699]; difference, -3.0 percentage points; 95% confidence interval, -5.9 to -0.1).” — the paper

A time-to-event analysis agreed: about a 31% lower rate of hospitalization or death (hazard ratio 0.69; 95% CI 0.48 to 1.01). There was 1 death in the molnupiravir group and 9 in the placebo group. Some subgroups leaned the other way: in patients with evidence of previous infection, low baseline viral load, or diabetes, the point estimate favored placebo.

Adverse events were reported in 30.4% of the molnupiravir group and 33.0% of the placebo group.

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

Early treatment with molnupiravir reduced the risk of hospitalization or death in at-risk, unvaccinated adults with Covid-19. In the full analysis the absolute reduction was about 3 percentage points, roughly half of what the interim analysis suggested.

Limitations

Only unvaccinated adults were enrolled. The benefit narrowed from the interim to the full analysis, and the upper end of the confidence interval came close to no effect. Several subgroups, some small, did not appear to benefit (previous infection, low baseline viral load, diabetes). There was an imbalance in sex between the groups at baseline.

What changed in practice

MOVe-OUT gave clinicians a second oral antiviral for high-risk outpatients, but a less potent one. In practice, molnupiravir became a fallback when nirmatrelvir plus ritonavir or remdesivir is not suitable, for example because of drug interactions or access.
