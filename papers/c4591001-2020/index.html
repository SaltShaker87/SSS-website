C4591001: Safety and Efficacy of the BNT162b2 mRNA Covid-19 Vaccine

background.txt

Background

By late 2020, SARS-CoV-2 had infected tens of millions of people, and safe, effective vaccines were needed urgently. BNT162b2 is a lipid nanoparticle-formulated, nucleoside-modified RNA vaccine that encodes a prefusion-stabilized, membrane-anchored version of the full-length SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. C4591001 was its pivotal efficacy trial: could a never-before-licensed vaccine platform prevent Covid-19, and was it safe?

methods.txt

Methods

DesignOngoing multinational, placebo-controlled, observer-blinded pivotal efficacy trial (phase 2/3 portion) at 152 sites in the United States, Argentina, Brazil, South Africa, Germany, and Turkey; 1:1 randomization.
PopulationPeople 16 years of age or older, including those with stable chronic medical conditions. 43,548 participants underwent randomization; 21,720 participants received BNT162b2 and 21,728 participants received placebo.
InterventionTwo doses of BNT162b2 (30 µg per dose), given intramuscularly 21 days apart.
ComparatorTwo doses of saline placebo, 21 days apart.
Primary endpointEfficacy against laboratory-confirmed Covid-19 with onset at least 7 days after the second dose, and safety (reactogenicity and adverse events).
Follow-upA median of 2 months after the second dose for the main safety data set; the trial was designed to follow participants for 2 years.

Efficacy was estimated with a Bayesian model, which is why the interval around the headline number is a credible interval rather than a confidence interval.

results.xls

Results

There were 8 cases of Covid-19 with onset at least 7 days after the second dose in the vaccine group and 162 in the placebo group. That works out to 95% vaccine efficacy (95% credible interval 90.3 to 97.6), and similar efficacy (generally 90 to 100%) held across subgroups by age, sex, race, ethnicity, body-mass index, and coexisting conditions.

“There were 8 cases of Covid-19 with onset at least 7 days after the second dose among participants assigned to receive BNT162b2 and 162 cases among those assigned to placebo; BNT162b2 was 95% effective in preventing Covid-19 (95% credible interval, 90.3 to 97.6).” — the paper

Protection started early. Between the first and second doses, efficacy was already 52% (39 vs 82 cases). Of the 10 cases of severe Covid-19 after the first dose, 9 were in placebo recipients and 1 in a vaccine recipient.

Reactogenicity was mostly short-lived and mild to moderate. Injection-site pain was the most common local reaction: 83% of younger participants after dose 1 and 71% of those older than 55. Fatigue and headache were the most common systemic events, more frequent after dose 2 (younger recipients: 59% and 52%), though many placebo recipients reported them too (23% and 24%). Serious adverse events were similar: 0.6% versus 0.5%.

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

A two-dose regimen of BNT162b2 conferred 95% protection against Covid-19 in people 16 years of age or older, and safety over a median of 2 months was similar to that of other viral vaccines.

Limitations

With about 19,000 participants per group in the main safety subset, the trial could not reliably detect less common adverse events. Follow-up was a median of 2 months after dose 2, so longer-term safety and the duration of protection were unknown. The report does not address whether vaccination prevents asymptomatic infection, and the high efficacy left too few breakthrough cases to establish a correlate of protection. Keeping a placebo group for the planned 2 years became ethically and practically impossible once the vaccine was authorized.

What changed in practice

C4591001 was the first phase 3 efficacy result for an mRNA vaccine, and it led straight to emergency authorization and mass vaccination in late 2020. It proved the mRNA platform at scale and set the template for rapid, large, event-driven vaccine trials.
