EPIC-HR: Oral Nirmatrelvir for High-Risk, Nonhospitalized Adults with Covid-19

background.txt

Background

By late 2021, outpatients at high risk of severe Covid-19 had few good options: monoclonal antibodies had to be given by infusion or injection in a health care setting and could lose activity against new variants. Nirmatrelvir is an oral inhibitor of the SARS-CoV-2 main protease (Mpro) with broad activity against human coronaviruses in the lab. It is paired with low-dose ritonavir, a pharmacokinetic enhancer that keeps nirmatrelvir levels up. EPIC-HR tested whether a 5-day oral course could keep high-risk patients out of hospital.

methods.txt

Methods

DesignPhase 2-3, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial (1:1) across sites in the United States, Europe, Latin America, Africa, and Asia.
PopulationSymptomatic, unvaccinated, nonhospitalized adults with confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection, symptom onset no more than 5 days before randomization, and at least one risk factor for progression to severe Covid-19. 2246 patients underwent randomization (1120 patients nirmatrelvir plus ritonavir, 1126 patients placebo).
InterventionNirmatrelvir 300 mg plus ritonavir 100 mg by mouth every 12 hours for 5 days.
ComparatorMatching placebo every 12 hours for 5 days.
Primary endpointCovid-19-related hospitalization or death from any cause through day 28, in patients treated within 3 days of symptom onset who had not received and were not expected to receive monoclonal antibodies (modified intention-to-treat population).
Follow-upEfficacy through day 28; safety information collected through day 34.

The first key secondary end point repeated the primary comparison in patients who started treatment within 5 days of symptom onset. A planned interim analysis was run first; the final analysis is the headline below.

results.xls

Results

In the final primary analysis (treatment within 3 days of symptoms), 0.72% of the nirmatrelvir group versus 6.45% of the placebo group were hospitalized for Covid-19 or died through day 28: a difference of −5.81 percentage points (95% CI −7.78 to −3.84; P<0.001) and a 88.9% relative risk reduction.

“corresponding to a difference of -5.81 percentage points (95% CI, -7.78 to -3.84; P<0.001) and an 88.9% relative risk reduction in Covid-19-related hospitalization or death from any cause.” — the paper

The effect held at every look: the interim analysis showed 0.77% versus 7.01% (89.1% relative risk reduction), and patients who started within 5 days had 0.77% versus 6.31% (87.8% relative risk reduction). Across the trial there were 0 deaths with nirmatrelvir plus ritonavir and 13 with placebo.

Viral load at day 5 was lower with nirmatrelvir plus ritonavir (adjusted mean difference −0.868 log10 copies/mL when started within 3 days). Treatment-emergent adverse events were similar overall (22.6% vs 23.9%), serious adverse events were less common (1.6% vs 6.6%), and dysgeusia (5.6% vs 0.3%) and diarrhea (3.1% vs 1.6%) were more frequent with the active drug.

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

Treating symptomatic, high-risk, unvaccinated outpatients with nirmatrelvir plus ritonavir lowered the risk of progression to severe Covid-19 by 89% compared with placebo, without evident safety concerns. The benefit held whether treatment started within 3 days or within 5 days of symptoms.

Limitations

The trial enrolled only unvaccinated people (vaccinated high-risk patients were studied separately in EPIC-SR), and recruitment ended on December 9, 2021, so the absolute benefit in today's mostly vaccinated or previously infected patients may be smaller. Ritonavir carries important drug interactions: nirmatrelvir plus ritonavir is contraindicated with certain drugs because of the risk of serious adverse events, and others need dose adjustment or a pause.

What changed in practice

EPIC-HR made oral nirmatrelvir plus ritonavir a first-line treatment for high-risk outpatients with mild-to-moderate Covid-19, started as early as possible and within 5 days of symptoms. It moved early treatment from infusion chairs to a pharmacy pickup, with a drug-interaction check as the key safety step.
