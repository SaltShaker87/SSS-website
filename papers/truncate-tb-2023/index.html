TRUNCATE-TB: An 8-Week Treatment Strategy for Rifampin-Susceptible Tuberculosis

background.txt

Background

Drug-susceptible tuberculosis is treated with the same 6-month rifampin-based regimen for almost everyone, even though many people would be cured with less. That means overtreating the majority to prevent relapse in a minority, and long courses strain both patients and programs. TRUNCATE-TB tested a different idea: treat everyone briefly with a potent 8-week regimen, extend treatment for anyone still clinically sick, monitor after treatment, and retreat the few who relapse. The question was whether this strategy, not just a regimen, could match standard care.

methods.txt

Methods

DesignAdaptive, open-label, multigroup noninferiority trial at clinics in Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand, Uganda, and India. Four strategy groups had different 8-week initial regimens; noninferiority was assessed in the two groups that completed enrollment.
PopulationAdults with rifampin-susceptible pulmonary tuberculosis; 674 participants were in the intention-to-treat population, and only 0.6% withdrew consent or were lost to follow-up.
InterventionStrategy: an 8-week initial regimen, either high-dose rifampin plus linezolid or bedaquiline plus linezolid (each with isoniazid, pyrazinamide, and ethambutol), extended for persistent clinical disease, then monitoring and retreatment (at least 24 weeks of standard treatment) for relapse.
ComparatorStandard treatment: rifampin and isoniazid for 24 weeks, with pyrazinamide and ethambutol for the first 8 weeks.
Primary endpointComposite of death, ongoing treatment, or active disease at week 96. Relapse that was retreated and resolved by week 96 counted as part of the strategy, not as a failure.
Follow-up96 weeks.

“The noninferiority margin was 12 percentage points.” The paper reports 97.5% confidence intervals to adjust for multiplicity (two strategy groups were each compared with standard treatment).

results.xls

Results

A primary-outcome event occurred in 3.9% of the standard-treatment group, 5.8% of the bedaquiline-linezolid strategy group, and 11.4% of the rifampin-linezolid strategy group.

“11 of the 189 participants (5.8%) in the strategy group with an initial bedaquiline–linezolid regimen (adjusted difference, 0.8 percentage points; 97.5% CI, −3.4 to 5.1; noninferiority met).” — the paper

So the bedaquiline-linezolid strategy met noninferiority (adjusted difference 0.8 percentage points; 97.5% CI −3.4 to 5.1), while the rifampin-linezolid strategy did not (adjusted difference 7.4 percentage points; 97.5% CI 1.7 to 13.2).

The payoff was time on treatment. Mean total treatment duration, including any extension and retreatment, was 180 days with standard care, 106 days in the rifampin-linezolid strategy, and 85 days in the bedaquiline-linezolid strategy. Grade 3 or 4 adverse events and serious adverse events were similar across the three groups.

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

A strategy of initial treatment with an 8-week bedaquiline-linezolid regimen was noninferior to standard 6-month treatment for tuberculosis with respect to clinical outcomes at week 96, with a much shorter total duration of treatment and no evident safety concerns.

Limitations

The trial was open-label, though standardized assessments, a prespecified outcome algorithm, and negligible attrition limited bias. No participants with HIV were enrolled, so the strategy needs testing in that group. Clinics were mainly in Asia. The strategy depends on close follow-up to catch relapse and on retreatment of at least 24 weeks for those who relapse, which programs must be able to deliver. Bedaquiline is a key drug for drug-resistant TB, so using it for drug-susceptible disease raises stewardship questions.

What changed in practice

TRUNCATE-TB reframed TB treatment as a strategy rather than a fixed 6-month course: treat briefly, extend if needed, monitor, and retreat the minority who relapse. It did not change routine first-line treatment overnight, but it showed most people with TB can be cured in about 2 months, and it provides a framework for future short regimens and treatment-monitoring biomarkers.
