background.txt

Background

Covid-19 can cause diffuse lung damage, and much of the harm in severe disease seemed to come from the host's inflammatory response. Glucocorticoids might dampen that inflammation-mediated lung injury and so reduce progression to respiratory failure and death. But steroid evidence from related syndromes such as SARS, MERS, and severe influenza was weak, and there were real worries about delayed viral clearance. RECOVERY, a large pragmatic platform trial in U.K. hospitals, set out to answer the question quickly and at scale.

methods.txt

Methods

DesignRandomized, controlled, open-label adaptive platform trial comparing several possible Covid-19 treatments with usual care in U.K. hospitals. Patients were assigned in a 2:1 ratio to usual care alone or usual care plus dexamethasone.
PopulationHospitalized patients with clinically suspected or laboratory-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection and no medical history that might put them at substantial risk if they took part. 2104 patients were assigned to dexamethasone and 4321 patients to usual care.
InterventionDexamethasone 6 mg once daily, by mouth or intravenously, for up to 10 days (or until discharge if sooner), plus usual care.
ComparatorUsual standard of care alone.
Primary endpointAll-cause mortality within 28 days after randomization.
Follow-up28 days, with outcomes collected at sites and through linkage with routine data sources.

Outcomes were also analyzed by the level of respiratory support at randomization: invasive mechanical ventilation, oxygen only (with or without noninvasive ventilation), or no oxygen at all. That prespecified split turned out to be the story of the trial.

results.xls

Results

Mortality at 28 days was lower with dexamethasone: 22.9% versus 25.7% with usual care (age-adjusted rate ratio 0.83; 95% CI 0.75 to 0.93; P<0.001).

“Overall, 482 patients (22.9%) in the dexamethasone group and 1110 patients (25.7%) in the usual care group died within 28 days after randomization (age-adjusted rate ratio, 0.83; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.75 to 0.93; P<0.001).” — the paper

The benefit depended on how sick patients were at randomization. Among patients on invasive mechanical ventilation, mortality was 29.3% versus 41.4% (rate ratio 0.64). Among those on oxygen without invasive ventilation it was 23.3% versus 26.2% (rate ratio 0.82). Patients on no respiratory support saw no benefit: 17.8% versus 14.0% (rate ratio 1.19), a result the authors describe as consistent with possible harm.

Patients given dexamethasone also left hospital sooner (median 12 days vs 13 days), were more likely to be discharged alive within 28 days (rate ratio 1.10), and were less likely to progress to invasive mechanical ventilation (risk ratio 0.77).

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

In patients hospitalized with Covid-19, dexamethasone lowered 28-day mortality among those receiving invasive mechanical ventilation or oxygen alone at randomization, but not among those receiving no respiratory support. For ventilated patients the absolute reduction was 12.3 age-adjusted percentage points, about a one-third proportional reduction.

Limitations

The trial was open-label, although its primary outcome, death, is objective. Only essential data were collected, with no physiologic, laboratory, or virologic measures, so the mechanism of benefit cannot be studied here. This is the preliminary report of one comparison in a platform trial. Patients on no respiratory support saw no benefit, and the data are consistent with possible harm in that group.

What changed in practice

RECOVERY made dexamethasone the first drug shown to reduce Covid-19 deaths. The results were announced on June 16, 2020, and adopted into U.K. practice the same day. Low-dose dexamethasone for patients who need oxygen or ventilation became standard care worldwide, while steroids are avoided in hospitalized patients who need no respiratory support.
