DoxyPEP: Postexposure Doxycycline to Prevent Bacterial Sexually Transmitted Infections

background.txt

Background

Gonorrhea, chlamydia, and syphilis rates have climbed for years among men who have sex with men (MSM), and condom promotion and screening alone had not turned the curve. Doxycycline is cheap, well tolerated, and active against all three. Taking a single dose after sex, the way HIV PEP works, could prevent infections before they take hold. DoxyPEP tested that idea in people already in HIV care or on HIV PrEP who had recently had an STI.

methods.txt

Methods

DesignOpen-label, randomized (2:1) trial in two cohorts, run at sites in San Francisco and Seattle. An independent data and safety monitoring board stopped the standard-care groups early after both cohorts crossed the stopping boundary for effectiveness.
PopulationMSM and transgender women who had gonorrhea, chlamydia, or syphilis in the past year and were either taking HIV PrEP (PrEP cohort) or living with HIV (PLWH cohort). 501 participants were enrolled (327 participants PrEP, 174 participants PLWH).
InterventionDoxycycline 200 mg taken within 72 hours after condomless sex (doxy-PEP).
ComparatorStandard care without doxycycline.
Primary endpointIncidence of at least one bacterial STI (gonorrhea, chlamydia, or syphilis) per quarterly follow-up visit, with STI testing every 3 months.
Follow-upQuarterly visits; the primary antimicrobial-resistance outcomes tracked tetracycline resistance in N. gonorrhoeae and S. aureus.
results.xls

Results

In the PrEP cohort, an STI was diagnosed at 10.7% of quarterly visits with doxy-PEP versus 31.9% with standard care, an absolute difference of −21.2 percentage points and a relative risk of 0.34 (95% CI 0.24 to 0.46). The PLWH cohort looked almost the same: 11.8% versus 30.5% (relative risk 0.38).

“for an absolute difference of −21.2 percentage points and a relative risk of 0.34 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.24 to 0.46; P<0.001)” — the paper

All three infections fell. In the PrEP cohort the relative risks were 0.45 for gonorrhea, 0.12 for chlamydia, and 0.13 for syphilis. The effect was weakest for gonorrhea, which is often tetracycline-resistant already.

Safety was reassuring: 5 grade 3 adverse events and no serious adverse events were attributed to doxycycline. Resistance signals were mixed and small. Among the few gonorrhea cultures available, tetracycline-resistant gonorrhea was found in 5 of 13 in the doxycycline groups and 2 of 16 with standard care, and overall doxycycline-resistant S. aureus was similar (5% vs 4%). About 5 people need to use doxy-PEP to prevent one quarter with an STI.

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

The combined incidence of gonorrhea, chlamydia, and syphilis was about two thirds lower with doxycycline postexposure prophylaxis than with standard care, supporting its use among MSM and transgender women with recent bacterial STIs, regardless of HIV status.

Limitations

Adherence was hard to measure because it depended on recalled condomless sex and event-driven doses. Tetracycline susceptibility results were available for only 17% of gonorrhea end points, so the resistance data are thin. Fewer than 5% of participants were transgender women, the trial was open-label, and it ran in two West Coast cities, so acceptability and STI rates may differ elsewhere. It did not enroll cisgender women.

What changed in practice

DoxyPEP moved doxycycline after sex from an idea to guideline-backed practice: U.S. CDC guidance now recommends offering doxy-PEP to MSM and transgender women with a bacterial STI in the past year. The open questions are the ones the trial could not settle: long-term resistance in gonorrhea, S. aureus, and the gut microbiome.
