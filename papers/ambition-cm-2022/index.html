background.txt

Background

Cryptococcal meningitis is a leading cause of HIV-related death, and most of those deaths happen in sub-Saharan Africa. The WHO-recommended induction regimen, a week of daily amphotericin B deoxycholate plus flucytosine, works but causes anemia, kidney injury, and electrolyte problems, and a week of IV amphotericin with close lab monitoring is hard to deliver in many hospitals. Liposomal amphotericin B (AmBisome) is less toxic and stays in tissue for a long time. After promising phase 2 data, AMBITION-cm asked whether a single big dose could replace the week of daily infusions.

methods.txt

Methods

DesignPhase 3, randomized (1:1), open-label noninferiority trial in Botswana, Malawi, South Africa, Uganda, and Zimbabwe.
PopulationHIV-positive adults with cryptococcal meningitis. 844 participants were randomized; 814 participants formed the intention-to-treat population, and none were lost to follow-up.
InterventionA single dose of liposomal amphotericin B 10 mg/kg, plus 14 days of flucytosine 100 mg/kg/day and fluconazole 1200 mg/day.
ComparatorThe WHO-recommended regimen: 7 daily doses of amphotericin B deoxycholate 1 mg/kg/day plus flucytosine 100 mg/kg/day, then 7 days of fluconazole 1200 mg/day.
Primary endpointAll-cause mortality at 10 weeks.
Follow-up10 weeks; all participants were admitted for 7 days of inpatient monitoring.

“The primary endpoint was allcause mortality at 10 weeks with the trial powered to show non-inferiority at a 10% margin.”

results.xls

Results

Ten-week mortality was 24.8% in the AmBisome group and 28.7% in the control group. The absolute difference was −3.9%, with an upper one-sided 95% confidence limit of 1.2%, well inside the 10% noninferiority margin.

“The absolute difference in mortality was -3.9%, with an upper 1-sided 95% confidence interval of 1.2%.” — the paper

The single dose cleared the fungus from the cerebrospinal fluid as fast as the week of daily amphotericin (−0.40 vs −0.42 log10 CFU/ml/day).

It was also much easier on patients. Grade 3 or 4 adverse events fell from 62.3% to 50.0%, and life-threatening grade 4 events from 30.1% to 21.7%. The biggest difference was anemia: grade 3 or 4 anemia in 13.3% versus 39.1%, with blood transfusions in 7.6% versus 18.0%.

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

A single 10 mg/kg dose of liposomal amphotericin B, given with flucytosine and fluconazole, was noninferior to the WHO-recommended standard of care for HIV-associated cryptococcal meningitis and caused fewer adverse events. The authors call it a practical, easier-to-administer, and better-tolerated treatment.

Limitations

The trial was open-label, and managing critically ill people with advanced HIV was complex, although the primary end point (death) and the main laboratory safety end points were objective. Every participant got pre-emptive fluids and electrolytes, intensive blood monitoring, and 7 days in hospital, which many real-world settings cannot provide; that likely made the control regimen look safer than it would in routine care. Access to AmBisome and flucytosine remains a barrier.

What changed in practice

AMBITION-cm changed the global standard: a single high dose of liposomal amphotericin B with flucytosine and fluconazole is now the WHO-preferred induction regimen for HIV-associated cryptococcal meningitis. It removes a week of toxic daily infusions and may allow earlier discharge, and the authors suggest the same approach may be worth testing for histoplasmosis and talaromycosis.
