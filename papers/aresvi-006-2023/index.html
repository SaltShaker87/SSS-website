AReSVi-006: RSV Prefusion F Protein Vaccine in Older Adults

background.txt

Background

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) causes acute respiratory infection, lower respiratory tract disease, complications, and deaths in older adults, yet decades of vaccine attempts had failed and no RSV vaccine was licensed. The breakthrough was structural: stabilizing the RSV fusion (F) protein in its prefusion shape, which exposes the epitopes targeted by neutralizing antibodies and is now recognized as important for a potent response. AReSVi-006 tested a single dose of an AS01E-adjuvanted prefusion F vaccine (RSVPreF3 OA) in adults 60 and older.

methods.txt

Methods

DesignOngoing, international, randomized (1:1), placebo-controlled phase 3 trial; vaccination before the RSV season.
PopulationAdults 60 years of age or older, including those with stable chronic conditions. 24,966 participants received a dose (12,467 participants vaccine, 12,499 participants placebo).
InterventionOne intramuscular dose of RSVPreF3 OA (RSV prefusion F protein with the AS01E adjuvant).
ComparatorPlacebo (saline).
Primary endpointEfficacy against RT-PCR-confirmed RSV-related lower respiratory tract disease during one RSV season. Success required the lower limit of the confidence interval to be above 20%.
Follow-upMedian 6.7 months for efficacy; safety through 6 months and beyond.
results.xls

Results

Over a median of 6.7 months, there were 7 cases of RSV lower respiratory tract disease in the vaccine group and 40 in the placebo group (1.0 vs 5.8 per 1000 participant-years): vaccine efficacy 82.6% (96.95% CI 57.9 to 94.1), comfortably clearing the 20% bar.

“vaccine efficacy against RT-PCR–confirmed RSV-related lower respiratory tract disease was 82.6% (96.95% confidence interval [CI], 57.9 to 94.1), with 7 cases (1.0 per 1000 participant-years) in the vaccine group and 40 cases (5.8 per 1000 participant-years) in the placebo group.” — the paper

Efficacy was 94.1% against severe RSV lower respiratory tract disease and 71.7% against any RSV acute respiratory infection. It was similar for RSV A and RSV B (84.6% and 80.9% against lower respiratory tract disease), across age groups, and in people with coexisting conditions.

The vaccine was more reactogenic than placebo, mostly with transient, mild-to-moderate reactions: injection-site pain in 60.9% versus 9.3%, fatigue in 33.6% versus 16.1%. Serious adverse events were similar (4.2% vs 4.0%).

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

A single dose of the RSVPreF3 OA vaccine had an acceptable safety profile and prevented RSV-related acute respiratory infection, lower respiratory tract disease, and severe lower respiratory tract disease in adults 60 years of age or older, regardless of RSV subtype and coexisting conditions.

Limitations

Few participants were 80 or older or frail, and the trial could not detect rare side effects. This report covers one RSV season; durability and the value of revaccination needed longer follow-up. The trial ran during the second year of the Covid-19 pandemic, when public health measures reduced RSV spread and shifted the season earlier, so the investigators enlarged the trial and started vaccinating in May.

What changed in practice

AReSVi-006 led to the first licensed RSV vaccine for older adults (Arexvy) in 2023. Together with the bivalent RSVpreF vaccine trial (RENOIR), it made RSV vaccination a routine conversation for adults 60 and older, with guidance focused on those at higher risk of severe disease.
