HPTN 084: Long-Acting Cabotegravir for HIV Prevention in Women

background.txt

Background

Oral pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) works when it is taken, and it has been introduced in more than 70 countries. But women face real barriers to a daily pill, including stigma, judgement, and the fear of violence, and in sub-Saharan Africa women and girls account for most new HIV infections. Long-acting cabotegravir, an integrase inhibitor given as an intramuscular injection every 8 weeks, had already outperformed oral PrEP in men and transgender women in HPTN 083. HPTN 084 asked the same question in cisgender women.

methods.txt

Methods

DesignPhase 3, randomized (1:1), double-blind, double-dummy, active-controlled superiority trial at 20 clinical research sites in seven countries in sub-Saharan Africa.
PopulationHIV-negative people assigned female at birth, aged 18-45 years, at risk of HIV, reporting at least two episodes of vaginal intercourse in the previous 30 days, and using long-acting reversible contraception. 3224 participants were enrolled (1614 participants cabotegravir, 1610 participants TDF-FTC).
InterventionCabotegravir 600 mg intramuscular injection at weeks 5 and 9 and every 8 weeks thereafter (after a 5-week oral lead-in), plus daily oral TDF-FTC placebo.
ComparatorDaily oral tenofovir disoproxil fumarate plus emtricitabine (TDF-FTC) plus placebo injections.
Primary endpointIncident HIV infection (intention-to-treat); safety: grade 2 or higher clinical and laboratory adverse events.
Follow-upMedian 1.2 years in the blinded phase, which was stopped early after the efficacy result.

Everyone got a placebo of the other product (“double-dummy”), so participants and staff could not tell which arm they were in. Participants who stopped injections were offered open-label daily TDF-FTC for 48 weeks.

results.xls

Results

There were 40 incident HIV infections over 3898 person-years: 4 in the cabotegravir group (0.2 per 100 person-years) and 36 in the TDF-FTC group (1.85 per 100 person-years). The hazard ratio was 0.12 (95% CI 0.05 to 0.31; p<0.0001), with a risk difference of −1.6%.

“four in the cabotegravir group (HIV incidence 0.2 cases per 100 person-years [0.06-0.52]) and 36 in the TDF-FTC group (1.85 cases per 100 person-years [1.3-2.57]; hazard ratio 0.12 [0.05-0.31]; p<0.0001; risk difference -1.6% [-1.0% to -2.3%].” — the paper

Why the gap? Adherence. In a random subset of TDF-FTC participants, only 42.1% of plasma samples showed tenofovir levels consistent with daily use, whereas injection coverage was 93% of person-years.

Adverse events were similar between groups except injection-site reactions, which were more common with cabotegravir (38.0% vs 10.7%) but did not lead anyone to stop injections. Confirmed pregnancy incidence was 1.3 per 100 person-years, with no congenital anomalies reported.

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

Both products were generally safe, well tolerated, and effective, but injectable cabotegravir every 8 weeks was superior to daily oral TDF-FTC in preventing HIV infection in women.

Limitations

The blinded phase was stopped early, which limited blinded safety data over 24 months. In one of the four infections in the cabotegravir group, retrospective testing showed HIV was already present at enrollment, and detection of HIV infection was delayed in several participants, highlighting how hard conventional HIV testing is with potent long-acting PrEP. Viral load testing to catch infections early may be a barrier to rollout in low- and middle-income settings.

What changed in practice

Together with HPTN 083, HPTN 084 established long-acting injectable cabotegravir as a PrEP option that does not depend on a daily pill, and it was the first trial to show an injectable superior to oral PrEP in women. It shifted prevention programs toward offering choice, especially for people for whom daily pills are hard to take or to hide.
