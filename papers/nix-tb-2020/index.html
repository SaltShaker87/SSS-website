Nix-TB: Bedaquiline, Pretomanid, and Linezolid (BPaL) for Highly Drug-Resistant Tuberculosis

background.txt

Background

Extensively drug-resistant tuberculosis (XDR-TB) had few treatment options and dismal results: in South Africa, published treatment success averaged 14%. Regimens for drug-resistant TB ran 18 months to over 2 years, used up to 7 drugs including injectables, and caused moderate to severe adverse events in many patients. Nix-TB tested something radically simpler: three oral drugs with strong anti-TB activity and little pre-existing resistance, bedaquiline (B), pretomanid (Pa), and linezolid (L), for just 26 weeks.

methods.txt

Methods

DesignOpen-label, single-arm study at three South African sites. There was no randomized control group, because no standard regimen for XDR-TB existed and outcomes were so poor.
Population109 patients patients with XDR-TB, or MDR-TB that was treatment-intolerant or not responding to treatment; 51.4% were living with HIV.
InterventionBedaquiline 400 mg daily for 2 weeks then 200 mg three times weekly, plus pretomanid 200 mg daily, plus linezolid 1200 mg daily (adjusted for toxicity), all by mouth for 26 weeks. The first 44 participants started linezolid as 600 mg twice daily.
ComparatorNone (single-arm). Context came from historical outcomes in South Africa.
Primary endpointUnfavourable outcome (treatment failure or relapse) 6 months after the end of treatment; favourable meant clinical resolution and negative culture with no prior unfavourable classification.
Follow-upTo 6 months after the end of treatment for the primary end point.
results.xls

Results

In the intention-to-treat analysis, 98 of the 109 patients patients (90%; 95% CI 82.7 to 94.9%) had a favourable outcome 6 months after finishing treatment. That included 89% of patients with XDR-TB and 92% of those with MDR-TB.

“In the intent to treat analysis, 98 patients (90%), (95% CI 82.7-94.9%) had a favourable outcome at 6 months after the end of treatment.” — the paper

There were 6 deaths early in treatment; otherwise unfavourable outcomes were rare: one withdrawal, one death during follow-up without relapse, two relapses (one followed by death), and one loss to follow-up.

The cost was linezolid toxicity. Peripheral neuropathy affected 81% of patients and myelosuppression 48%; 57% had grade 3 or higher adverse events. These were manageable but often required linezolid dose reductions or interruptions: only 15% completed 26 weeks of 1200 mg daily without an interruption or reduction.

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

BPaL is a viable option for tuberculosis patients with highly resistant forms of TB, provided adequate safety management is available. The authors note that treatment success of 90% in this historically hard-to-treat group was in the range of standard therapy for drug-susceptible TB.

Limitations

There was no randomized control group, so the comparison with historical outcomes is indirect, and bedaquiline and linezolid were themselves improving outcomes in routine care during the study. The trial was small and run at three South African sites. Linezolid toxicity was common at 1200 mg daily, which left the right dose and duration as open questions.

What changed in practice

Nix-TB led to the approval of pretomanid as part of BPaL and changed drug-resistant TB treatment from years of injections to 6 months of pills. The linezolid toxicity it exposed drove the follow-up ZeNix trial of lower doses and shorter linezolid courses, and BPaL-based regimens (BPaLM) are now WHO-recommended for MDR/rifampicin-resistant TB.
