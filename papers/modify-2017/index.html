MODIFY I and II: Bezlotoxumab for Prevention of Recurrent Clostridium difficile Infection

Wilcox MH, Gerding DN, Poxton IR, et al. N Engl J Med 2017;376(4):305-317. PMID 28121498

background.txt

Background

C. difficile is the most common cause of infectious diarrhea in hospitalized patients in high-income countries, and recurrence after antibiotic treatment is common. Antibiotics treat the episode but keep disrupting the gut flora that would normally hold C. difficile in check. Actoxumab and bezlotoxumab are human monoclonal antibodies that neutralize toxin A and toxin B. MODIFY I and II tested whether a single infusion, added to standard antibiotics, could prevent the next episode.

methods.txt

Methods

DesignTwo double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled phase 3 trials at 322 sites in 30 countries (2011 to 2015). Randomization was stratified by the standard-of-care antibiotic and by hospitalization status.
Population2655 adults adults receiving oral standard-of-care antibiotics (metronidazole, vancomycin, or fidaxomicin) for primary or recurrent C. difficile infection.
InterventionA single 60-minute IV infusion of bezlotoxumab 10 mg/kg, or actoxumab plus bezlotoxumab (10 mg/kg each). An actoxumab-alone arm in MODIFY I was stopped after a planned interim analysis.
ComparatorPlacebo infusion, on top of the same standard-of-care antibiotics.
Primary endpointRecurrent infection (a new episode after initial clinical cure) within 12 weeks after the infusion, in the modified intention-to-treat population.
Follow-up12 weeks after infusion.

77% of participants had at least one risk factor for recurrence or a bad outcome, such as age 65 or older, a previous episode, compromised immunity, or severe infection.

results.xls

Results

In both trials, recurrence was lower with bezlotoxumab than with placebo: 17% versus 28% in MODIFY I (adjusted difference −10.1 percentage points) and 16% versus 26% in MODIFY II (adjusted difference −9.9 percentage points). Adding actoxumab gave similar reductions (−11.6 percentage points and −10.7 percentage points) without improving efficacy.

“(MODIFY I: 17% [67 of 386] vs. 28% [109 of 395]; adjusted difference, −10.1 percentage points; 95% confidence interval [CI], −15.9 to −4.3; P<0.001; MODIFY II: 16% [62 of 395] vs. 26% [97 of 378]; adjusted difference, −9.9 percentage points; 95% CI, −15.5 to −4.3; P<0.001)” — the paper

Bezlotoxumab did not change how many people got better from the current episode (initial clinical cure 80% with bezlotoxumab, 80% with placebo). What it changed was what came next: sustained cure, meaning cure without recurrence over 12 weeks, was 64% versus 54% (actoxumab plus bezlotoxumab: 58%).

The number needed to treat to prevent one recurrence was 10 overall, and 6 among participants aged 65 or older and those with a previous episode. Adverse event rates were similar across groups; the most common were diarrhea and nausea.

conclusions.txt

Conclusions

Among people receiving antibiotics for primary or recurrent C. difficile infection, a single infusion of bezlotoxumab was associated with a substantially lower rate of recurrence than placebo and had a safety profile similar to placebo. Adding actoxumab did not improve efficacy.

Limitations

The standard-of-care antibiotic was chosen by investigators rather than standardized (randomization was stratified by it, and the effect did not depend on it). The timing of the infusion relative to symptom onset varied widely. Severity at baseline is probably underestimated, because most participants were already on antibiotics when it was assessed. Other prevention strategies such as fecal microbiota transplantation were not allowed, so combined effects are unknown, and the number of people exposed to bezlotoxumab was too small to detect rare harms.

What changed in practice

MODIFY made toxin neutralization a recurrence-prevention tool: bezlotoxumab was approved and later recommended as an adjunct for patients with recurrent C. difficile or a high risk of recurrence. It mattered most for older patients and those with prior episodes (number needed to treat 6). Prescribing information later added caution in patients with heart failure, and the manufacturer has since discontinued the product, but MODIFY remains the proof that preventing recurrence is possible without changing the antibiotic.
