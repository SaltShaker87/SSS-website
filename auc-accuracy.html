A plain-language record of a check run on 10 November 2026, for residency programs that are considering Assessments Under Curve (AUC) for their Clinical Competency Committee (CCC).

What was being checked

AUC (Assessments Under Curve) is a local web app for an internal medicine residency Clinical Competency Committee. Programs import evaluations from MedHub, keep committee notes, and use a local artificial intelligence (AI) model to draft milestone summaries aligned to the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME). Version 1.1.0 adds review periods and advisor packets. All data stays on the local machine. The language model runs locally through Ollama. Nothing in this check required sending resident comments off the computer that hosts AUC.

A review period is the date range a summary covers for one resident, ending on a cutoff date. The period starts the day after that resident's previous packet. If they have no previous packet, it starts the day after the last CCC meeting in the current academic year at which they were discussed. If neither of those applies, it starts on 23 June. A MedHub comment counts toward the period if the rotation it belongs to ended in that range. An "All time" view reproduces the older behaviour, with no period filter.

Advisor packets are prepared on the Packets page. The committee picks a meeting date (the cutoff defaults to 7 days before that date), adds the presenting advisors (their advisees come with them), and can optionally press "Randomize within each advisor." Randomization may be used once per meeting and cannot be undone. Changing a resident's group by hand requires a reason. "Generate all" then builds one packet at a time in the background. A packet can later be withheld, with a reason. Withheld packets are removed from that advisor's downloadable zip file, but the committee can still download them one at a time.

Each packet is a PDF file. The header lists the resident, their postgraduate year (PGY) at the cutoff, the advisor, the review period, and counts of comments, evaluators, and rotations. It also carries the label "AI generated draft. Not reviewed or edited." For each of the six ACGME competencies, the packet shows a count of MedHub milestone ratings plus Strengths and Growth areas backed by word-for-word quotations, or the wording "Not enough evidence in this review period." A "Previously" block and a 21 sub-competency appendix follow. A footer appears on every page. Study codes are not printed. Residents with few comments also get a caution: "Few evaluations were available for this period."

A safeguard runs before a statement is kept: if its supporting quotes are not exact copies of the source comments, the statement is dropped.

How the test was run

Machine health was checked with preflight.sh. Model fitness was checked with check-model.sh and the --show-output flag, which uses only made-up test comments. The live features were then exercised on a private copy of the program's database, not on the copy the committee uses day to day. The copy matched the original (26 tables, 631 rows). It was opened as a second instance of the app on a separate port and was deleted after the check.

Machine health

Preflight reported 24 checks passed, 1 warning, and 0 failures. The warning was "No database yet." It is harmless in this setup. When preflight is run by hand it does not read the app's settings file, so it looks for the database in the default folder inside the app. The real database was in the configured data folder, and the running app was using it.

Model fitness

The model under test was nemotron-3.5-bolt:latest. The check-model script ran three sub-competency generations and two advisor-packet competency generations, the same way the app does. Results:

The 1.8-minute figure is an estimate for a packet that has comments for the model to write about. It is the figure to plan around when residents have a full period of evaluations. It is not what a batch will take when most packets have no comments.

Resident page

On the test copy, the current review period showed sensible dates and counts, and those counts matched what was in MedHub for the same dates. A warning about a stale MedHub import displayed correctly. Generating a summary with MedHub comments only finished all 21 sections. Sixteen of those sections said there was no evidence in the period. That was expected: the resident chosen for this check had few evaluations. Switching the period picker to "All time" still showed the full history.

Advisor packets

Creating a meeting auto-filled the cutoff 7 days before the meeting date. Adding presenting advisors brought their advisees with them. "Randomize within each advisor" split those advisees across the two groups (in one example, 2 versus 1 out of 3, which is as even as three advisees can be). The button locked after it was used. Changing a group by hand prompted for a reason.

Pressing "Generate all" produced 37 packets in about 3 minutes, with 0 failures. That is much faster than the 1.8-minute estimate above because most residents had no comments yet in the period, so the model had little or nothing to write and many packets completed in seconds. Once residents have a full period of evaluations, a batch of this size would be expected to take much longer. Planning should use the check-model estimate of about 1.8 minutes per advisor packet, or about 2 minutes per resident with comments.

Validation log

After generation, the summary validation log was read for counts only. It sits in the data directory at logs/summary_validation.log. The log recorded 1 dropped quote (a quote that was not an exact copy, which the safeguard is meant to catch), 0 competency sections discarded, and 0 truncations. No section was cut off for being too long, which is consistent with this model's context window being large enough for these packets.

Limitations

This is a record from one residency program, on one test day, on one machine. Most residents had little MedHub data in the period, which is expected early in the academic year. That means the batch was a strong test of empty-packet handling and of the workflow, and a weaker test of long, comment-rich packets. Timing for residents with a full set of evaluations is an estimate from the check-model script, not a measurement from this batch.

The packet review was done by one physician, who is also the developer. An independent reviewer at another program may weigh wording differently. Programs that adopt AUC should run the same checks on their own data, with their own model choice, before they rely on packets in a meeting or in a study.
