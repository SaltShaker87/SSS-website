    Staphylococcus aureus resistance mechanisms and treatment options

    From mecA to MIC creep: how resistance guides the right drug for the right isolate

    Case

    A 58-year-old man with diabetes and a chronic foot ulcer presents with fever, hypotension, and a hot left thigh after a shaving cut. Vancomycin plus piperacillin-tazobactam is started after blood cultures. Overnight the lab calls: gram-positive cocci in clusters in both bottles. By morning the organism is Staphylococcus aureus, susceptibilities pending. Should you stay on vancomycin, and what will the resistance report mean?

    By the end of this talk you will be able to
    • Explain major S. aureus resistance mechanisms — penicillinase, PBP2a, and vancomycin and daptomycin phenotypes.
    • Distinguish MSSA, MRSA, VISA, VRSA, and inducible clindamycin resistance on a report.
    • Choose targeted therapy for bacteraemia, skin infection, and pneumonia caused by MSSA and MRSA.
    • Apply source control, duration, and consultation principles for invasive disease.

    Why Staphylococcus aureus still matters on call

    Staphylococcus aureus colonises skin and nares in about one in three people and turns pathogenic when the barrier breaks or a device is present. It causes cellulitis, bacteraemia, endocarditis, osteomyelitis, and device infection. The wrong empiric or definitive antibiotic directly raises mortality, and resistance is the key to that choice.

    Resistance in S. aureus is simpler than in many gram-negatives: destroying the drug, changing the target, and thickening or remodelling the wall explain most reports. Cure also needs source control and penetration to the site.

    The beta-lactam story: from penicillinase to PBP2a

    Beta-lactams kill by binding penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) that cross-link peptidoglycan. S. aureus defeats them two ways.

    First is beta-lactamase via blaZ, which hydrolyses penicillin G and ampicillin but not anti-staphylococcal penicillins or cephalosporins. Most isolates now produce it, so plain penicillin rarely works unless blaZ absence is confirmed.

    Second is altered target: mecA (rarely mecC) on the staphylococcal cassette chromosome mec (SCCmec) encoding PBP2a, an alternative PBP with very low beta-lactam affinity. The lab reports this as methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) versus methicillin-susceptible S. aureus (MSSA). The name is historical — we no longer use methicillin — but MRSA means resistant to all conventional beta-lactams except ceftaroline. Oxacillin resistance implies resistance to cefazolin, ceftriaxone, cefepime, and piperacillin-tazobactam as well.

    SCCmec types I–III were classic hospital strains and type IV–V community strains often susceptible to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX), but this distinction has blurred. Assume MRSA is multidrug-resistant until proven otherwise.

    Pearl

    MRSA means PBP2a is present. Treat as resistant to nafcillin, oxacillin, cefazolin, ceftriaxone, and piperacillin-tazobactam even if the panel infers otherwise.

    The vignette patient grows S. aureus reported as oxacillin-resistant. Which beta-lactams remain reliable?

    Oxacillin resistance signals mecA/PBP2a with low affinity for all standard beta-lactams. Ceftaroline is the only beta-lactam with reliable MRSA activity and is not empiric for undifferentiated bacteraemia.

    When vancomycin and daptomycin become less reliable

    Vancomycin, a glycopeptide, binds the D-alanine-D-alanine terminus of the wall precursor. Resistance is two distinct phenotypes.

    VISA and hVISA — vancomycin-intermediate S. aureus — have a minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of 4 to 8, no vanA, and a thickened wall that traps drug. Heterogeneous VISA (hVISA) may test susceptible. Both arise with prolonged vancomycin and persistent bacteraemia. An MIC of 2 with slow clearance should prompt discussion rather than a higher dose.

    VRSA — vancomycin-resistant S. aureus (MIC ≥16) — acquires vanA from enterococci. Vancomycin has no activity. Only a handful of cases exist in the United States. Involve infection control and public health immediately.

    Daptomycin is bactericidal for bacteraemia but is inactivated by surfactant — never for pneumonia. Resistance via mprF is uncommon and often travels with VISA.

    Common pitfall

    Chasing an MIC of 2 with higher vancomycin does not rescue efficacy and raises nephrotoxicity. If bacteraemia persists 3–4 days on appropriate dosing, reassess source control and discuss alternatives.

    Pearl

    Thick wall hurts two drugs at once. When VISA or hVISA is suspected, expect vancomycin to underperform and verify daptomycin MIC.

    Other phenotypes that change the prescription

    Three other reports mislead. First, inducible clindamycin resistance: an isolate may appear susceptible but carry erm, methylating the ribosome after erythromycin exposure. Labs perform the D-test; if positive, report clindamycin as resistant. Do not use it for invasive disease when D-test positive.

    Second, TMP-SMX and doxycycline often remain active against community MRSA for uncomplicated skin infection, but not bacteraemia or endocarditis — high failure in blood.

    Third, linezolid resistance via cfr or 23S mutation follows prolonged exposure. Linezolid penetrates lung and is an option for MRSA pneumonia, but watch for thrombocytopenia and serotonin syndrome.

    Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL) testing does not guide therapy.

    Background

    Mupirocin resistance via mupA matters for decolonisation, not acute therapy. Do not add nasal mupirocin to treat bacteraemia.

    A skin abscess grows MRSA, erythromycin-resistant, clindamycin-susceptible, D-test positive. No bacteraemia. How do you use clindamycin?

    Positive D-test signals inducible erm. Induction causes failure despite low MIC. Choose TMP-SMX or doxycycline if susceptible.

    Choosing therapy: MSSA is not treated like MRSA

    Cover MRSA empirically when S. aureus is possible, then narrow. Definitive vancomycin for MSSA when a beta-lactam is possible raises mortality.

    For methicillin-susceptible S. aureus (MSSA) invasive disease, an anti-staphylococcal beta-lactam beats vancomycin. Guideline-derived adult regimens, to confirm with pharmacy, include nafcillin or oxacillin 2 g intravenously (IV) every 4 hours, or cefazolin 2 g IV every 8 hours (weight-based, more frequent for severe or central nervous system disease). Cefazolin is preferred outside the central nervous system for safety; nafcillin or oxacillin remain preferred for central nervous system infection.

    For methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA) invasive disease, vancomycin remains first-line. Dosing now targets an area under the curve (AUC) to MIC of 400–600, not a fixed trough, with pharmacy-guided loading and levels. For vancomycin intolerance or persistent bacteraemia, daptomycin 8–10 mg per kg IV once daily is an alternative for bacteraemia and right-sided endocarditis (higher than 6 mg per kg for S. aureus). Daptomycin is inactivated in lung — never for pneumonia.

    Other options are syndrome-specific. Ceftaroline covers MRSA for salvage. Linezolid 600 mg every 12 hours covers pneumonia. TMP-SMX and doxycycline are oral only for uncomplicated skin infection when bacteraemia is excluded. Clindamycin only if D-test negative. Vancomycin plus piperacillin-tazobactam is more nephrotoxic than vancomycin plus cefepime; stop the second agent once MSSA is confirmed.

    Pearl

    For MSSA, beta-lactams beat vancomycin. Switch off vancomycin the same day MSSA returns unless a true severe allergy prevents it.

    Common pitfall

    Using daptomycin for MRSA pneumonia because the MIC says susceptible. Surfactant inactivates it. Choose vancomycin or linezolid.

    Blood cultures grow MSSA; the patient is on vancomycin, no allergy, normal renal function. Best next step?

    Beta-lactams kill MSSA faster with lower mortality than vancomycin. Switch to a single beta-lactam; dual therapy adds toxicity without benefit.

    On the wards: culture, source, heart, and duration

    Antibiotics are one quarter of bacteraemia care. Repeat cultures, source control, and endocarditis evaluation matter as much.

    Repeat blood cultures every 48 hours until clearance. Persistence at 3–4 days predicts complicated disease. Remove implicated catheters, drain abscesses, and plan early hardware removal.

    Every bacteraemia needs endocarditis consideration. Transthoracic echo is the minimum; transoesophageal echo is for high risk, persistent bacteraemia, prosthetic valves or inadequate images. Infectious diseases consultation improves outcomes — consult early.

    Duration follows complexity. Uncomplicated bacteraemia — clearance in 2–4 days, no endocarditis or metastatic foci, no prosthetic material, source controlled — is at least 14 days from first negative culture, IV days only. Complicated disease needs 4–6 weeks or longer.

    Background

    Nasal colonisation raises infection risk, but universal decolonisation with mupirocin and chlorhexidine is for intensive care and perioperative protocols, not routine ward bacteraemia without infection control direction.

    Pearl

    Repeat, remove, image, consult. Repeating cultures, removing source, imaging the heart, and calling infectious diseases improves survival more than tweaking troughs.

    What not to do

    Keep empiric vancomycin plus piperacillin-tazobactam short. The pair is more nephrotoxic than vancomycin plus cefepime. De-escalate within 48–72 hours as cultures return.

    A single bottle of S. aureus is not a contaminant. Unlike coagulase-negative staphylococci, treat even one bottle as true bacteraemia: repeat cultures and do not dismiss it. Draw two sets before antibiotics when possible.

    Do not treat colonisation — a nasal MRSA swab or colonised wound without infection needs no systemic therapy. Do not add rifampin alone; its role is limited to select device infections after clearance. Clarify penicillin allergy labels — many patients can safely receive cefazolin.

    Common pitfall

    A positive MRSA nasal polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is not infection, and a negative nasal PCR has strong negative predictive value for MRSA pneumonia. Use it to stop vancomycin, not to start it for every fever.

    Pearl

    S. aureus in one bottle is bacteraemia until proven otherwise. Look hard for the source and repeat cultures.

    Which empiric pair for MRSA plus pseudomonal coverage is most associated with acute kidney injury?

    Vancomycin plus piperacillin-tazobactam raises acute kidney injury versus vancomycin plus cefepime in multiple studies. Pair vancomycin with cefepime when you need both and stop the second agent quickly.

    The five-minute version

    • MRSA means mecA-encoded PBP2a. Treat as resistant to all conventional beta-lactams — do not switch among them.
    • MSSA bacteraemia needs an anti-staphylococcal beta-lactam (nafcillin, oxacillin, or cefazolin), not vancomycin.
    • VISA/hVISA is thickened wall trapping vancomycin; VRSA is vanA-mediated high-level resistance and rare. Both often raise daptomycin MIC.
    • Never use daptomycin for pneumonia; check D-test before clindamycin; avoid oral TMP-SMX or doxycycline for bacteraemia.
    • For any S. aureus bacteraemia: repeat cultures to clearance, remove source, image heart, consult infectious diseases. Treat at least 14 days from first negative culture if uncomplicated, 4–6 weeks if complicated.
